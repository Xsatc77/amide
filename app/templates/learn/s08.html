You open the box and find small glass vials with a rubber top and a metal cap. Inside is a puff of white powder, or what looks like a tiny disc.

The Vial

  • Small glass bottle, sealed under vacuum. Often 2 mL to 10 mL in size, though it holds far less than that.
  • The rubber stopper is what you pierce with a needle. It stays clean only if you wipe it first.
  • The metal cap is a seal. Pop off the plastic flip-top and you'll find the stopper under it.

The Powder

  • It's lyophilized, or freeze-dried. Water was removed so the peptide can sit on a shelf.
  • The "cake" can look surprisingly small. A tiny amount can still be the correct amount.
  • The milligrams on the label are the intended amount. As in section 6, that's a claim, not a measurement.

Turning It Into Something You Can Use (Reconstitution)

  1. You add liquid to the powder (see the next section).
  2. The powder dissolves into a clear solution.
  3. The strength depends on one simple relationship:

concentration = amount of peptide ÷ amount of water

Twice the water gives half the strength. Amide's calculator does the arithmetic and tracks the vial for you.

Storing It

  • Unmixed powder: keep it cold and dark unless the instructions say otherwise.
  • Mixed solution: keep it refrigerated, and don't shake it.
  • How long it lasts is the part nobody can promise. The "28 days" you'll see quoted is the usual pharmacy rule for an opened multi-dose vial of a manufactured product, and it can never be longer than the expiry date. A peptide you mixed yourself may stay good for less, and the right answer is different for different compounds. Amide uses 28 days as a default reminder, not as proof the contents are fine.
  • Cloudy, discolored, or floating particles: throw it out.
