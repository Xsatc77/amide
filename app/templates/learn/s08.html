Good news: nothing goes anywhere near your back. "BAC" stands for bacteriostatic, and this water goes into the vial.

What it is

Sterile water with a small amount of preservative (0.9% benzyl alcohol). "Bacteriostatic" means it slows the growth of bacteria. It does not make a dirty needle or an unwiped stopper safe, so you still have to keep everything clean.

Why it's used

The preservative is what lets a vial be used more than once over a few weeks. Plain sterile water has no preservative and is meant to be used once.

Which water?

WaterInject it?
Bacteriostatic water, sealed, labeled for injectionYes, for this purpose
Sterile water for injectionYes, single use
Saline or nasal rinseNo, unless labeled and sold for injection
Tap, bottled or distilled waterNever

Who should be careful

Benzyl alcohol is generally avoided in newborns, and some people are sensitive to it. If that might apply to you, ask a clinician, and read the wording in the package insert of the product you have.

Mixing it without wrecking it

  1. Wash your hands and wipe the top of both vials with an alcohol pad. Let it dry.
  2. Draw up the water.
  3. Insert the needle into the powder vial and let the water run slowly down the inside wall of the glass. Don't blast it onto the powder.
  4. Swirl gently. Don't shake. Wait for it to turn clear.
  5. Label it with the date you mixed it.

Afterward

Refrigerate the mixed peptide. A bottle of BAC water that has been opened also has a limited life, so write the date on it. In Amide the BAC bottle gets its own entry and a note that it is not refrigerated.

Bacteriostatic water is not for your back, your bath, or the bottle of tap water in your kitchen. It's for the vial, and only a sealed bottle labeled for injection will do.
