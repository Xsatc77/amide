Start with an honest list of both.

What people hope to get

  • Lower cost than a pharmacy product, or access to something a pharmacy doesn't offer.
  • Interest in compounds still being studied.
  • A sense of control over their own health.

These are the reasons people give, not proven results. For many compounds the human evidence is thin, early, or from animals only. A forum post is not a study.

What can go wrong

  • Wrong contents. A different compound, a mix, or nothing useful. Independent testing has found vials that held none of the named compound at all.
  • Wrong amount. More or less than the label says. A vial's powder weight can include water and salts, so it may hold less actual peptide than the printed milligrams.
  • Impurities. Leftover chemicals from manufacturing, or other peptides made by mistake.
  • Contamination. Bacteria or endotoxin (fragments of bacteria) in something you inject. Endotoxin can make you quite sick even when no bacteria are alive.
  • No safety net. No monitoring system for side effects, no recall, and no one required to tell you when a batch was bad.
  • Unknowns. Long-term effects, and how a compound interacts with your medications or conditions.
  • Money, legal and sport risk. Packages get seized, vendors vanish, some compounds are controlled in some places, and several are banned for tested athletes (section 11).

Before you go further, ask yourself

  • Am I pregnant, nursing, or trying to be?
  • Do I have a condition, or take a medication, that could interact?
  • Do I have someone to call, and a plan, if I react badly? (Section 10.)
  • Would I still do this if the vial turned out to be useless? What if it turned out to be harmful?

If any answer worries you, that's a reason to talk to a clinician first.
