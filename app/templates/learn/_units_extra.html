How much peptide those units hold depends on how strong your mix is (its concentration):

peptide drawn = (units ÷ 100) × concentration

Worked examples (made-up numbers, to show the arithmetic only. They are not dose suggestions.)

Same vial, two different mixes. A vial holds 10 mg of peptide.

  • Mixed with 2 mL of water, it is {{ num(a.concentration_mg_ml, 2) }} mg/mL. Then 10 units ({{ num(a.volume_ml, 2) }} mL) holds {{ num(a.mg) }} mg, which is {{ num(a.mcg, 0) }} mcg.
  • Mixed with 1 mL of water, it is {{ num(b.concentration_mg_ml, 2) }} mg/mL. Then the same 10 units holds {{ num(b.mg) }} mg.

That is the whole point: the mark on the syringe is identical, but you have drawn twice as much peptide.

Working backward. You want to draw 0.5 mg from a 2.5 mg/mL mix (5 mg in 2 mL): 0.5 ÷ 2.5 = {{ num(0.5 / 2.5, 2) }} mL, which is {{ num(units_for(0.5, 5, 2), 0) }} units.

Three traps

  1. Units are not milligrams. "10 units" says nothing about how much peptide you drew until you know the concentration.
  2. mg and mcg: 1 mg is 1,000 mcg. A slip of one decimal place is a tenfold error.
  3. Check the label on your syringe. This assumes U-100, but some syringes are marked in mL only, and a few (such as some veterinary insulin syringes) are U-40, where 40 units is 1 mL.

When you mix a vial, check your numbers with Amide's calculator and do not rely on mental arithmetic.
