This tutorial won't give you a list of vendors. A list would be out of date within a month, and it would read as an endorsement. What it can teach is how to look.

Where People Look

  • Online communities and discussion groups.
  • Messaging groups where vendors post price lists.
  • Independent testing groups, where members buy and test products themselves.

Read Everything With Suspicion

  • Affiliate links. Many "reviews" earn the reviewer a commission. A glowing post with a discount code is advertising.
  • Fake accounts. A crowd of brand-new accounts all praising one seller is a warning sign.
  • Social-media storefronts. A seller who exists only as a profile, with no history and no way to verify who they are, is the riskiest kind.
  • Pressure. "Limited stock," "buy before the price changes," and "DM me for a private deal" are all reasons to slow down.

What Helps

  • Communities that share lab results for specific products and update them over time.
  • People who tell you the bad outcomes as well as the good ones.
  • Sellers whose reputation you can trace across more than one independent group.
